May-26, Case 1, drawn from the actual paper. The questions never depend on each other; each one sits on one or two paragraphs.

Every case has one question from each subject: FR, AFM, Audit, Law, Direct Tax, GST/Customs, Costing/Strategy. The company and the industry are new every time. The question types are not.

02

How to read a case in 10 minutes

  1. Read the 8 questions first (2 min). Write the subject in the margin next to each. Now you know which paragraphs matter before you've read them.
  2. Read short, numeric paragraphs slowly; skim long story paragraphs. In both papers checked, the 10-line paragraph with a few numbers and a "ordered / plans to / payment due" sentence was worth 8–12 of 25 marks. The 50-line company history was worth 2–5.
  3. Circle hinges. A person, date or deadline mentioned early that a later paragraph quietly relies on ("CA L is the internal auditor" is what makes the GST paragraph exist). They connect two subjects and carry 7–9 marks.
  4. Box reused numbers. Anything with a currency, a date and a rate together. One such number fed three questions from three subjects.
  5. When you see 3–4 similar numbers, write the rule before computing. Four exchange rates were given; one was right. The mark is for knowing which date picks which rate.
03

The index you carry

A book's index runs the wrong way

It goes name of the law → page. The paper never gives you the name of the law. It gives you facts. In the hall you need facts → page. That is what these sheets are.

Seven sheets, one per subject

Each line: 2–3 facts you'll recognise → section / standard → the one rule that picks the right number. Built from 120 past questions. The same shapes come back with new company names.

What one line looks like

GST  advance received · invoice after supply · rate changed between → s.14 CGST, GST book p.__ rule: 2 of 3 events (supply / invoice / payment) before the change → old rate
Customs  FOB in foreign currency · several exchange rates · BoE date ≠ arrival date → s.14 + s.15 Customs, p.__ rule: exchange rate = CBIC on BoE date; duty rate = later of BoE / arrival

04

In the hall

The 60-second lookup

question stem → subject → scan that paragraph → match 2–3 facts on the sheet → read page + rule → open the book at that page → strike out the decoy numbers → write.

Three kinds of material

Trigger sheets (7 pages, front of binder) — every question.
Procedure sheets for AFM, DT, GST, Customs, FR — the dense numeric paragraphs.
Framework lists for Strategy — BMC 9, Porter 5, BSC 4, Kano, 7S.

Order is free. The 8 questions never depend on each other. Skipping one costs only that one.
Don't re-read the case when one answer feels wrong. A misread paragraph affects at most 1–2 questions. Fix that one, move on.
Which case to drop: the one where the same numbers feed questions from the most different subjects — slowest per mark. (Seen on two cases; use as a tie-breaker.)
